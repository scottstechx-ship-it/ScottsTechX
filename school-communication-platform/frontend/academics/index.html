Two levels, one standard

Kalinabiri Secondary School is a government-aided mixed day school with between 800 and 1,000 learners studying S.1 to S.6. Learners move from the broad lower-secondary programme into focused A-Level specialisation, with subject choice guidance from the Director of Studies.

O-Level — S.1 to S.4

Lower secondary · UCE

The lower-secondary curriculum builds a strong foundation across languages, mathematics, sciences, social studies and vocational subjects, with continuous assessment that has been competency-based under Uganda's lower secondary reforms.

  • Compulsory core: English, Mathematics, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Geography, History & Political Education, CRE/IRE, ICT
  • Practical work in the science laboratories every week
  • Continuous assessment (BOT/MOT) recorded and reported each term
  • Supervised preparation and remedial support for candidates
  • UNEB registration for the Uganda Certificate of Education in S.4

A-Level — S.5 to S.6

Upper secondary · UACE

At A-Level learners take three principal subjects with a subsidiary subject and general paper, chosen from the science and humanities tracks offered here. Classes are smaller and teaching is geared to university entry and professional courses.

  • Science combinations: PCM, BCM, PEM, PCB, BCFN, BCA and PMTD, all with ICT or Sub-Math
  • Arts & humanities combinations: MEG, MEE and BCG with ICT
  • Subsidiary subjects and General Paper for all candidates
  • UNEB registration for the Uganda Advanced Certificate of Education in S.6
  • Career guidance and university application support

Teaching, assessment and reporting

Every learner's progress is tracked all term, not only in the end-of-term examination.

In class

  • Schemes of work and lesson plans reviewed by heads of department
  • Weekly practicals in the science laboratories
  • ICT lab sessions for computer studies and research
  • Library and reading periods
  • Remedial and holiday classes for candidate classes

Assessment

  • Beginning-of-term (BOT) and mid-of-term (MOT) tests
  • End-of-term examinations with graded report cards
  • Continuous assessment marks combined with exam marks
  • Class position and subject comments on each report card
  • UNEB registration and mock examinations for candidates

Reporting to parents

  • Termly report cards with attendance records
  • Fee statements and receipts from the school office
  • Attendance alerts when a learner is absent or late
  • Class teacher contact for any subject concern
